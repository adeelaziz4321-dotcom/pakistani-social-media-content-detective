An exploratory study of what social-media content appears to attract engagement for Pakistani fashion and retail brands.

Business question: “What type of social-media content gets Pakistani audiences to engage?”

1. Executive summary

This project examined post-level metrics and a collected sample of audience comments. Reels make up most of the posts in this dataset. Among posts with both likes and comment counts, Reels had a median of 3,371 recorded interactions (likes + comments). A few very popular image posts had much higher counts, but there were only four comparable images. Creator- and celebrity-associated posts also appear among the most-liked examples.

The evidence supports a testable content idea, not a universal rule: try a short, culturally relevant creator-led product Reel, then compare it with a product-focused version. The dataset cannot fairly establish which platform is best, whether sentiment causes engagement, or which audience demographic responds most.

2. Data and method

The analysis uses the supplied posts.csv and comments.csv. evidence.csv provides comment examples, and recommendations.csv contains heuristic suggestions that should be treated as provisional—not as independently verified findings.

  • Compared likes and comments across the available platforms and post formats.
  • For the format comparison, calculated recorded interactions as likes + comments_count for posts with both values; reported the median to reduce the influence of viral outliers.
  • Summarized the supplied manual sentiment and topic labels. Labels marked uncertain or provisional were not treated as verified truth.
Important: Recorded interactions are not an engagement rate. Reach, impressions, and consistently measured audience size are not available for most posts.

Hashtags, timing, and audience segments

  • Hashtags: The hashtag field is sparse. #wearyourself and #khaadi each appear three times, while #readytowear appears twice. There is not enough consistent data to identify a winning hashtag.
  • Timing: Posting time is blank for 10 posts, and four post dates are missing. Day-of-week counts alone do not measure performance; comparable reach and posting conditions are missing.
  • Audience: Comments include English/mixed language, Roman Urdu, Urdu script, and emoji-only responses. Demographic details such as age, location, and gender are not supplied, so audience segments cannot be reliably identified.

4. Recommendation: what should the brand post next?

Recommended test: Publish a short Instagram Reel showing a new Pakistani fashion collection through a creator or recognizable local personality. Use a simple story or styling transformation, clearly show the product, and finish with a useful CTA such as “View the collection” or “Check price and availability.”
  1. Create two comparable versions for the same product and campaign: (A) creator-led storytelling/styling and (B) a product-focused demonstration.
  2. Keep the platform, campaign period, offer, and paid support as similar as possible. If practical, test versions with comparable audience groups.
  3. Record reach, impressions, likes, comments, shares, saves, link clicks, and purchases for each post.
  4. Compare engagement per reach—(likes + comments + shares + saves) ÷ reach—along with clicks and conversions. Do not select a winner from raw likes alone.
  5. Review comments for price and availability questions, then make that information easy to find in the caption or linked product page.

5. Conclusion

In this dataset, Reels are the most common format and have a median of 3,371 recorded likes-plus-comments among posts with both metrics. Several top posts combine collection content with a recognizable collaborator, making creator-led storytelling a sensible experiment. The evidence is exploratory: uneven platform coverage, small format samples, missing reach and timing data, and uncertain comment labels prevent stronger claims.

Answer to the business question: Creator-led collection Reels are a promising format to test with this sample, but the brand should validate the idea using reach-adjusted results and a controlled comparison before treating it as a proven strategy.
Data note: Counts and labels are taken from the supplied CSV files. Comments may be a collected sample rather than every comment on each post. “Likes + comments” is a descriptive interaction count, not a standardized engagement rate or a measure of sales.
